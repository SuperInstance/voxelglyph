The projection

What survives

Two colours in one luma class are not “hard to tell apart”. They are the same input. No model recovers a distinction the representation never carried.

Then the pool

403 apart in RGB is about 20 apart after a 2×4 block average — comparable to the quantisation step. The loss is not only the projection; it is the pooling, and the pooling is what buys the determinism.

The ceiling, before any data exists

Enumerating the RGB cube and bucketing by luma finds the blind spot directly. Eight materials drawn from that one class have a brightness-only accuracy ceiling of 1/8 — independent of architecture, dataset, and training budget.

A first attempt at this experiment failed usefully: eight carefully chosen “chromatically different” materials produced no collisions and a ceiling of 1.0000, which demonstrated nothing. The blind spots are not something you trip over by choosing colours carefully. You have to go looking for them.

Honest status

SOLID

The luma ceiling

Reproducible, and the ceiling is a property of the encoder rather than of any model. The demo above runs the same code.

INCONCLUSIVE

The distillation

All three encodings land at or below a majority-class baseline. Three instruments lied on the way to that number — degenerate labels, unequal input widths, and a “3-layer MLP” that was linear. Below baseline here means undertrained, not unreadable.

The question worth asking next

Not how much does brightness lose — that is answered. It is how much brightness can you afford to keep alongside material identity. The 2×4 pool is what buys the compactness and the byte-identical determinism, so the answer is probably a mixed channel rather than a swap.

The measurable version: find the smallest per-cell symbol set for which a linear policy matches a pixel policy’s accuracy. No renderer required.
